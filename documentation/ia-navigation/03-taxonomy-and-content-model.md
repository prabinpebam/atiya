# Taxonomy and content model

## Taxonomy principles

- Use a small controlled vocabulary.
- Separate what a page **is** from what it is **about**.
- Allow multiple practice areas per case study.
- Prefer audience language over internal organization language.
- Treat tools as supporting metadata, not primary categories.
- Add a label only when it improves retrieval or comprehension.

## Controlled vocabularies

### Practice area

Every case study has one primary practice area and up to two secondary practice areas.

- Product Experience
- Systems & Technology
- Motion & Spatial
- Strategy & Leadership

### Contribution

Use verbs that make responsibility explicit. Select all that materially apply.

- Led
- Directed
- Strategized
- Researched
- Facilitated
- Designed
- Prototyped
- Built
- Mentored

Avoid vague labels such as "involved in" or "helped with."

### Outcome type

Use one or two:

- Product outcome
- Customer outcome
- Business outcome
- System outcome
- Organizational outcome
- Team capability
- Practice innovation

### Engagement type

- Shipped product
- Product direction
- Design system
- Prototype or experiment
- Workshop or sprint
- Strategy initiative
- Team or organization development
- Film, motion, or spatial piece

### Visibility

- Public
- Public with redactions
- Summary only
- Private discussion only
- Not publishable

Only the first three belong on the public site.

## Core content types

### Case study

Required fields:

- Title
- One-sentence premise
- Short summary
- Primary practice area
- Secondary practice areas
- Contribution labels
- Outcome types
- Engagement type
- Time period, at an appropriate level of precision
- Context or organization, if public
- Team and collaborators
- Personal role
- Constraints
- Outcome statement
- Visibility status
- Cover or representative media with text alternative
- Narrative sections
- Related content

Optional fields:

- Normalized or relative metrics
- Testimonial, with permission
- Tools
- Public external links
- What changed afterward
- What would be done differently

### Practice-area page

- Name
- Short promise
- Point of view
- Capabilities
- Typical questions addressed
- Selected case studies
- Related practice areas
- Optional notes

### Leadership topic

- Principle or topic
- Why it matters
- Observable behaviors
- Routines or mechanisms
- Evidence or example
- Failure modes or trade-offs
- Related case studies

### Note

- Title
- Summary
- Publication and updated dates
- Topic labels
- Body
- Related work or expertise

## Metadata limits

For any visible case-study card, show no more than:

- one primary practice area;
- one concise role phrase;
- one outcome phrase;
- optionally, year or period.

Full metadata can appear inside the case study. This prevents the interface from recreating the original flat-list problem as a wall of tags.
