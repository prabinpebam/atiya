# Editorial and governance rules

## Voice

Use a voice that is:

- direct rather than promotional;
- specific rather than filled with design jargon;
- confident about contribution without claiming team output as individual output;
- reflective about trade-offs and limitations;
- understandable to design, engineering, product, and executive audiences.

## Preferred language

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "I led..." followed by scope | "I owned everything..." |
| "The team..." for collective work | Using "I" for team output |
| Specific decision and consequence | Generic process claims |
| "AI-augmented workflow" with an explanation | "AI-powered" without substance |
| "Production frontend" when code shipped | "Developer" based only on prototypes |
| "Selected work" | "All projects" |
| Relative or approved metrics | Unsupported numerical claims |

## Case-study quality rules

Each published case study must:

- state the personal role near the beginning;
- distinguish individual, team, and organizational contributions;
- identify at least one meaningful constraint or trade-off;
- explain important decisions, not merely activities;
- include an outcome statement;
- remain useful when images fail to load;
- have permission for every included quote and non-public artifact;
- link to at least one relevant practice area.

## AI disclosure

Disclose AI use where it materially affected the method, result, or interpretation. Describe:

- the problem AI helped address;
- the human direction, judgment, and verification involved;
- boundaries or risks;
- the resulting change in speed, quality, scope, or collaboration.

Do not list AI as a capability solely because a general-purpose assistant was used during routine writing or coding.

## Label governance

- The four practice-area names are controlled labels.
- New visible tags require a retrieval or comprehension need.
- Synonyms should map to an existing canonical label.
- Review labels twice a year or after five new content items, whichever comes first.
- Renaming a label requires updating navigation, metadata, cross-links, and redirects together.

## Content lifecycle

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Draft | Incomplete and not reviewed |
| Fact review | Contributions, outcomes, dates, and permissions being verified |
| Editorial review | Structure, clarity, and narrative being reviewed |
| Approved | Safe and ready to publish |
| Published | Live and included in navigation |
| Stale | Accurate but needs review or updated framing |
| Archived | Removed from active navigation but retained or redirected |

## Review cadence

### Quarterly

- Check contact and resume links.
- Check external links.
- Confirm current role and positioning.
- Review Now, if present.

### Twice yearly

- Review featured case-study order.
- Review taxonomy usage and empty categories.
- Recheck confidential or employer-related content.
- Confirm leadership pages still reflect current practice.

### Annually

- Reassess audience priorities and positioning.
- Archive weak or redundant content.
- Run a lightweight navigation and accessibility evaluation.
- Review privacy and analytics disclosures.

## Ownership

Even for a personal site, assign an explicit owner for:

- factual accuracy;
- confidentiality and permissions;
- editorial quality;
- accessibility;
- broken links and redirects;
- taxonomy changes.

The same person may hold all roles, but the checks should remain distinct.
