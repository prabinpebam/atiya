# Goals, audiences, and journeys

## Communication goals

The site should help a visitor understand, within a short first visit, that the portfolio owner:

- is a design leader who remains close to craft and technology;
- can lead work from ambiguity through shipped outcomes;
- operates across product experience, systems, emerging technology, motion, and spatial media;
- builds teams and improves how organizations use design;
- can explain decisions, trade-offs, and impact clearly.

## Audience priorities

| Priority | Audience | What they need to learn | Likely path |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hiring executives and design leaders | Leadership scope, judgment, strategic influence, outcomes | Home → Leadership → featured case study → About |
| 1 | Hiring managers and interview panels | Role, process, collaboration, depth, shipped impact | Home → Work → case study → related expertise |
| 2 | Product, engineering, and business peers | How design connects to delivery, technology, and business | Home → Expertise → Systems & Technology → case study |
| 2 | Designers and potential team members | Craft standards, culture, coaching, and ways of working | Home → Leadership → Team Culture → Work |
| 3 | Event organizers and professional peers | Distinct point of view and areas of authority | Home/About → Notes or selected expertise → Contact |

## Top visitor questions

The IA should answer these questions without requiring a visitor to reconstruct a career history:

1. What kind of design leader is this person?
2. What outcomes have they influenced?
3. What did they personally lead, decide, make, or enable?
4. How broad is the practice, and where is the deepest expertise?
5. How do they work with design, product, engineering, and leadership?
6. How do they build teams and culture?
7. How are AI and code used as part of the design process?
8. What can be discussed publicly, given confidentiality constraints?
9. How can I contact them or review a concise resume?

## Core journeys

### Journey A: Leadership-first

1. Arrive on Home.
2. Read positioning and representative impact.
3. Open Leadership.
4. Review leadership principles, scale, and one leadership case.
5. Continue to a related product or systems case study.
6. Review About or resume.

Success condition: the visitor can describe the leadership style and cite evidence of organizational influence.

### Journey B: Work-first

1. Arrive directly on a case study or open Work.
2. Scan the impact summary.
3. Understand role, collaborators, constraints, and outcome.
4. Read the deeper narrative.
5. Follow related case studies or expertise links.

Success condition: the visitor understands contribution and impact without reading every page.

### Journey C: Capability-first

1. Open Expertise.
2. Choose one of four practice areas.
3. See a clear point of view, methods, and selected evidence.
4. Follow a related case study.
5. Move laterally to another practice area.

Success condition: breadth feels intentional and connected rather than like an unprioritized skill list.

### Journey D: Referral or search landing

1. Land on a deep page.
2. Identify the page type, author, and portfolio context.
3. Read a concise summary.
4. Navigate to Work, Expertise, Leadership, or About.

Success condition: every deep page works as a credible entry point without requiring Home first.

## Content hierarchy

When deciding prominence, use this order:

1. Outcomes and influence
2. Decisions and judgment
3. Role and collaboration
4. Process and methods
5. Artifacts and visual polish
6. Tools

Tools matter as evidence, but they should not define the site's structure.
