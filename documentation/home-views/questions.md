# Questions

Questions answers the nine questions hiring visitors ask, in their order and their words. Each answer is two or three lines, the capabilities it shows, and the evidence behind it. It meets the hiring manager where they already are, with questions, and it gives the honest answer to the one other views avoid: what can't be discussed publicly. It's part of [Home page views](README.md) and reads the [capability spine](foundation.md).

> **TL;DR.**
> - **The nine questions** from [the IA's goals](../ia-navigation/01-goals-audiences-and-journeys.md), on one list, with **Ask something else** under them.
> - **Question 1 is open at rest,** and its answer is the six capabilities (L1), so the map comes before any choice.
> - **An answer is the signal (L2):** two or three lines of fact, and the capabilities it shows. **Its evidence**, under it, is the detail (L3): three to five items, with Ask me about it where the work isn't public.
> - **On a phone,** the questions are an accordion: the first three, the open one, and "Five more questions".
> - **Ask something else** matches a typed question to the nine and the spine, in the browser, with no AI and no server; nothing matched opens the contact form with the question filled in.

## 1. The idea

A hiring manager reading a portfolio is running an interview in their head. The IA work wrote down the questions they ask, in order. Questions puts those questions on the page and answers each one the way a good candidate would: briefly, with a fact, and with the proof ready if asked. Its value is that the visitor doesn't need to know the owner's vocabulary (his six capability names) to find what they came for, and that the questions themselves show what kind of evidence the site has.

## 2. Who it serves

| Persona | How Questions serves them |
|---|---|
| Recruiter | Question 9 answers itself (résumé and contact); question 1 gives the six capabilities |
| Hiring manager | Their own questions, answered with evidence, in the order they'd ask them |
| Panel member | A link to a question (`#q6`), its answer and its evidence, to prepare an interview around |
| General audience | A readable Q&A, like an interview in a magazine |
| Returning visitor | The same questions; answers that grow as the content grows |

## 3. The three levels

| Level | In Questions | Revealed by |
|---|---|---|
| **L1, the map** | The nine questions; question 1's answer, the six capabilities | Nothing |
| **L2, the signal** | A question's answer: two or three lines, and the capabilities it shows | Pressing a question |
| **L3, the detail** | The answer's evidence: three to five items, each with its kind and source; an item expands to its full résumé line or quote | Under the answer; an item's own press |
| **Beyond** | The article, the résumé, the contact form | Links in the evidence |

## 5. Interaction

| Action | Result |
|---|---|
| Press a question | Its answer replaces the open one (wide), or opens under it (phone); the address becomes `#q<n>` |
| Next, at the end of an answer | The next question |
| Press an evidence item | It expands to its full résumé line or quote, or opens its page |
| Ask me about it | The contact form, with the question or the item as its topic |
| Ask something else | A field; as you type, it suggests the closest of the nine questions and the matching capabilities (§5.1) |
| Open `/views/questions/#q<n>` | Opens with that question's answer |

### 5.1 Ask something else

- **Matching in the browser:** the typed question is matched against the nine questions, the spine's capability names and keywords, their synonyms (shared with [Keywords](keywords.md)) and the pages' titles. The index is built into the page; nothing typed leaves the browser.
- **What it shows:** the closest question's answer, or the matching capabilities' evidence, with "Closest answer:" above it so it never pretends to be a direct reply.
- **Nothing matched:** "There's no answer to that here yet." and Ask me about it, which opens the contact form with the question filled in.
- **No AI at launch.** A grounded AI answer (one that answers only from the site's public pages and cites them) would need a server the site doesn't have, and an answer the owner hasn't signed off. It's a later option (A2).

## 9. Accessibility

- **Without script, it's a plain Q&A:** all nine questions and answers are in the page's HTML, as headings and sections, linked from the list.
- **With script, on a wide screen:** a vertical tab list (the WAI-ARIA tabs pattern, manual activation: the arrow keys move, Enter or Space opens); on a phone, an accordion of buttons with `aria-expanded`.
- Opening an answer moves focus only when the visitor pressed a link or used the keyboard; the answer's heading is announced.
- Ask something else is a combobox with its suggestions in a listbox; "no answer here yet" is said in text.

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Answers read as marketing | Facts and word-for-word quotes only; two or three lines; the owner signs off |
| The visitor's question isn't one of the nine | Ask something else, and its honest no-match with Ask me about it |
| Question 8 draws attention to what's missing | It's the honest answer to a question every hiring manager has, and it turns each gap into an invitation to talk |
| Questions 2 and 4 are hard to answer with public facts | They point to the evidence that exists, and the content plan in [foundation §4](foundation.md) adds more |
