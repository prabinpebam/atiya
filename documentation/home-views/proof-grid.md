# Proof grid

Proof grid lays the profile out as a table: the six capabilities as rows, five kinds of evidence as columns (shipped, led, built, wrote, said), and in each cell the count of proof behind that claim. It's the view for the analytical hiring manager and the interview panel: every claim and what supports it, on one screen, with nothing to scroll past and nothing to miss. It's part of [Home page views](README.md) and reads the [capability spine](foundation.md).

> **TL;DR.**
> - **Every claim and its proof in one table:** six rows (L1), each with its proof line, against Shipped, Led, Built, Wrote and Said.
> - **A cell is the signal (L2):** its count, filled when there's a page or a recommendation on the site, outlined when it rests on résumé lines, empty when there's nothing yet.
> - **A cell opens its evidence under its row (L3);** a row's name opens all of it.
> - **On a phone,** the table becomes six rows, one open, each closed one saying how many proofs it has.
> - **The lowest miss risk of any view,** and low effort: the grid is computed from the spine. Its honesty is the point, and its risk: thin cells show.

## 1. The idea

Hiring managers are asked to justify a shortlist. A spec sheet does that: rows of claims, columns of evidence, cells that can be checked. The grid makes the profile's breadth (six rows), its depth (the counts) and its credibility (filled means you can read it yourself) visible in one look, and it answers question 4 of the IA ("How broad, and where is the depth?") without a word of prose. It's also the view least likely to fail the failure case: there is no capability, and no kind of evidence, that isn't on the first screen.

## 2. Who it serves

| Persona | How Proof grid serves them |
|---|---|
| Recruiter | Six capabilities at once; the counts as a quick measure of depth |
| Hiring manager | Every claim and its proof; where the depth is, and what to ask about |
| Panel member | The view to prepare from: a cell is exactly the evidence for "has he led AI work?" |
| General audience | Less inviting than the others; the voices strip and the sections below give them a way in |
| Returning visitor | The same table, with counts that grow as the owner adds work |

## 3. The three levels

| Level | In Proof grid | Revealed by |
|---|---|---|
| **L1, the map** | The six row names, and the five column names | Nothing |
| **L2, the signal** | Each row's proof line; each cell's count and fill | A glance |
| **L3, the detail** | A cell's evidence items (or a row's, all kinds), under the row | Pressing a cell or a row's name |
| **Beyond** | The article, the résumé, the recommendation | Links in the evidence |

## 4. Layout and composition

### 4.1 Wide screen (1280 px)

| Row | Content | Columns |
|---|---|---|
| Strip | The name; Read résumé, Contact me, Change view | 12 |
| Head | The positioning line, and the legend: filled, outlined, empty | 12 |
| The grid | A header row (Capability, Shipped, Led, Built, Wrote, Said), then six rows: the capability's name and proof line, and five cells | 12 (the capability column 4, the cells 8) |
| Evidence | When a cell is open: its items, full width, under its row | 12 |
| Voices | The three recommendations, one line each, with the person's name and title | 12 |
| Below | The site's sections; Other ways to explore | 12 |

The grid and its head fit in the first 800 px.

**The columns:**

| Column | What counts | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Shipped** | Products and features released to people | Microsoft Family |
| **Led** | Programs, teams and orgs he led | The AI transformation program for Design |
| **Built** | Things he made himself | Cloner Pro, Slate, Atiya |
| **Wrote** | His articles and essays | The best AI model won't win… |
| **Said** | Colleagues' recommendations | Diego Baca, March Roger, Prabu Ambravaneswaran |

**A cell** shows its count. It's **filled** (the indigo spot, paper number) when at least one of its items is a page or a recommendation on the site; **outlined** (with a small résumé mark beside the number) when all its items are résumé lines; **empty** (a faint dot) when there are none. The counts are computed from the spine's proof `kind`s, never typed.

### 4.2 Tablet (768 px)

The full table still fits: the capability column narrows, with the proof line under the name; the cells shrink to 44 px squares.

### 4.3 Phone (360 px)

## 5. Interaction

| Action | Result |
|---|---|
| Press a cell | Its evidence opens under its row: each item's title, its kind and its source (a page, a résumé line with Ask me about it, a recommendation's words) |
| Press a row's name | All of the row's evidence, grouped by kind |
| Press the open cell again, its close button, or Esc | Closes; focus returns to the cell |
| Arrow keys in the grid | Move between cells (one tab stop for the whole grid) |
| Open `/views/proof-grid/#cap-ai-led` | Opens with that cell's evidence open |
| Phone: press a closed row | It opens, and the open one closes |
| Print | The grid and its evidence print on one page |

## 6. Task flows

**Hiring manager checking AI depth** (about 1 minute)
1. Reads the AI row: Shipped 2 and Led 1 outlined, Built 3, Wrote 2, Said 1 filled.
2. Presses AI × Led: the AI transformation program, a résumé line, with Ask me about it.
3. Presses AI × Built: Cloner Pro, Watai, Khonjel. Opens Cloner Pro.
4. Presses Ask me about it on the program: the contact form, topic filled.

**Panel member preparing**
1. Opens the link the hiring manager sent, `/views/proof-grid/#cap-strategy-said`: March Roger's and Prabu Ambravaneswaran's words.
2. Prints the grid for the interview.

**Recruiter**
1. Reads the six rows and their proof lines; presses Read résumé.

## 7. Visual direction

- **A spec sheet with presence:** generous rows, the capability names in Fraunces, the proof lines in Newsreader, the column heads in Figtree's label style; hairline rules, no boxes around cells.
- **Two marks, not a heat map:** filled and outlined, with the count; no gradient of colours to decode.
- **The spot colour is the evidence you can read:** filled cells are the only indigo on the page, so the eye goes to what can be checked.
- **The voices sit under the grid** like the signatures on a document.

## 8. Motion

| Motion | When | Duration | Reduced motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell lift | Hover or focus | The site's short duration | The border darkens |
| The evidence opens | Pressing a cell | About 250 ms | Instant |
| Capability morph to another view | Change view | About 300 ms | Instant |

Nothing moves on its own.

## 9. Accessibility

- A real `<table>` with a caption ("Evidence for each capability"), column and row headers (`scope`), and a button in each non-empty cell.
- A cell's button says everything: "Design leadership, led: 5 items, 2 on the site". Fill is never the only cue: outlined cells also carry the résumé mark.
- One tab stop for the grid, arrow keys within it (a roving tabindex), Enter or Space to open; the evidence is a region named by its cell and follows its row in the reading order.
- On a phone, the accordion's rows are buttons with `aria-expanded`.
- The table never scrolls sideways from 320 px: under 600 px it becomes the accordion.

Offered with 3 kinds in use and 8 proof items ([§5](for-every-site.md)). Settings: Density, Order, Columns and Empty cells ([controls §3](controls.md)) |

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Thin cells show (AI's and Strategy's evidence is mostly résumé lines today) | That's honest, and it's the content plan's to-do list ([foundation §4](foundation.md)); every outlined cell offers Ask me about it |
| Empty cells read as weaknesses | Empty is a faint dot, not a red mark; some kinds simply don't apply (Making isn't "shipped" or "led") |
| It looks like a spreadsheet, not a first impression | The type, the voices and the strip carry the impression; it's an alternative, not the default |
| Counts invite comparing capabilities | Each capability's best evidence kind matters more than its total; the drawer leads with the strongest item |

## 12. Open decisions

| # | Decision | Options | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Empty cells | Show a faint dot; hide them | Show them: a full grid is easier to read, and it's honest |
| G2 | Counts or marks only | Counts; marks | Counts: they're the depth at a glance |
| G3 | The columns | The five kinds; add Outcomes | The five now; add Outcomes when there are measured outcomes that can be shown |
| G4 | The open row on a phone | The first; the lens's; none | The lens's, else the first |

## 13. As built (stage 4)

Proof grid is built and offered at `/views/proof-grid/`. The rows are the strengths the site shows, 3 to 6; the columns are the person's (Columns), else the five kinds of proof those strengths use most, in the kinds' own order. Every count is computed from the picks.

| Part | Where |
|---|---|
| The view | [`ProofGrid.astro`](https://github.com/prabinpebam/atiya/blob/main/src/site/components/compounds/ProofGrid.astro), with the lead and [`Voices.astro`](https://github.com/prabinpebam/atiya/blob/main/src/site/components/compounds/Voices.astro) in its slots; its story is in the design library |
| From the spine to the table | [`proofGrid.ts`](https://github.com/prabinpebam/atiya/blob/main/src/site/home/proofGrid.ts) (pure, unit-tested in `homeProofGrid.test.ts`): the columns, each cell's count and state, its button's name, and the evidence grouped by kind |
| Its behaviour | [`scripts/proofGrid.ts`](https://github.com/prabinpebam/atiya/blob/main/src/site/scripts/proofGrid.ts): the roving tabindex, opening and closing, Esc, the phone's accordion and telemetry; the `#cap-<id>` and `#cap-<id>-<kind>` addresses are read by [`homeAddress.ts`](https://github.com/prabinpebam/atiya/blob/main/src/site/scripts/homeAddress.ts), which Bento shares |
| Its sizes | The `c.proof-grid` tokens: the capability column, an evidence item's width (20 rem) and the container query at which it becomes the accordion (37.5 rem) |

Where the build differs from the design above:

- **The lead is a strip.** The person's name, role and actions sit in one compact row above the table (the lead's `strip` layout), so all six rows are in the first 800 px at 1280 wide and on a 360 by 740 phone.
- **The legend is in the caption,** on the table's name's line (under it when there's no room), so it's read with the table.
- **Compact rows:** a row's name and its proof line, then its cells, each 44 px. A strength's name keeps its last word and its chevron together when it wraps.
- **The phone (§4.3):** closed, a row is one 44 px line: its name and how much proves it ("4 proofs"). The first row is open on its kinds at rest, each a button with its count; a kind opens its evidence, and another row's name moves the open row there. The legend leaves out "None yet", since the accordion never shows an empty cell. The lens's row comes with stage 2c.
- **Its own evidence row,** under the row that opened it and the table's whole width, rather than Bento's drawer. A group per kind, each item with where it's from (on the site, on the résumé, in their words) and Ask me about it on a résumé line; Close and Esc give the focus back to what opened it.
- **Telemetry:** `capability_l3_open` with the strength's ID, `view: 'proof-grid'` and the kind (`all` for a row's name).
- **Print:** the whole table, with every row's evidence after it, and no chevrons or Close buttons.
- **Not built yet:** the role lens (stage 2c); Contact me filling in the topic for Ask me about it (the owner's decision).

Evidence: the E2E group "home views" (coverage at 1280 by 800 and 360 by 740 in both modes; one tab stop, the arrows between the cells, a cell opening its kind and a name all of it, Esc and Close, `#cap-` addresses; axe in both modes), "home views on a phone" (no sideways scroll and 44 px targets from 320 px with the evidence open; the accordion), the telemetry test in "home views: change view", the design system's unit tests, and the screenshots of the story and `/views/proof-grid/` in the `visual` project.
