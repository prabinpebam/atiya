# Spotlight

Spotlight shows one highlight at a time on a large stage: the picture, what it was and why it matters, and the way to its details. A rail under the stage lists every highlight, and a row above it names the six capabilities. It's a carousel designed against the carousel's known failures: nothing advances by itself, and nothing is hidden behind the next slide. It's part of [Home page views](README.md) and reads the [capability spine](foundation.md).

> **TL;DR.**
> - **One thing at a time, everything named:** the stage shows one of eight highlights; the **Jump to** row names all six capabilities (L1), and the rail shows all eight titles.
> - **The stage is the signal (L2):** a big picture, the title, its capability, two lines and Show the details. **Show the details** opens its evidence under the stage (L3).
> - **Nothing moves until the visitor moves it:** Previous, Next, a swipe, the arrow keys, the rail or Jump to. There's no autoplay, because almost no one clicks a slide that rotates on its own.
> - **On a phone:** the capability row, the picture, the panel and the rail scrolling sideways, all on the first screen.
> - **It shares its eight highlights with the Showreel,** so it costs little once those exist. It's the weakest of the seven views at L1, so it's offered, not the default.

## 1. The idea

A gallery's spotlight puts one piece on the wall with room around it, so it gets full attention. For a visitor who likes to look at one thing properly before moving on, that's a better first impression than a grid of equals. The usual carousel loses the visitor in two ways, and Spotlight closes both:
- **Rotation.** In Erik Runyon's measurements, about 1% of visitors clicked a rotating feature, and most of those clicks were on the first slide ([Runyon, 2013](https://erikrunyon.com/2013/07/carousel-interaction-stats/)). Nielsen Norman Group advises against auto-advancing and for clear, labelled controls ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-effective-carousels/)). Spotlight never advances by itself.
- **Hidden slides.** What's on slide 5 is invisible to anyone who doesn't reach it, which is the failure case exactly. Spotlight names every highlight on the rail and every capability in the Jump to row, so slide 5 is one press away and its name is already on screen.

## 2. Who it serves

| Persona | How Spotlight serves them |
|---|---|
| Recruiter | The Jump to row names the six capabilities; résumé and contact in the strip |
| Hiring manager | One piece of work at a time, with room for its picture and its point; the rail shows how much more there is |
| Panel member | A link opens a given highlight (`#h-family`) |
| General audience | The most picture-led view after Cover; browsing with Next feels like turning pages |
| Returning visitor | The rail shows what's new; the owner can add a highlight without changing the layout |

## 3. The three levels

| Level | In Spotlight | Revealed by |
|---|---|---|
| **L1, the map** | The Jump to row (six capabilities) and the rail (eight highlight titles) | Nothing |
| **L2, the signal** | The highlight on stage: picture, title, capability tag, two lines | Nothing for the first one; Next, the rail or Jump to for the others |
| **L3, the detail** | Show the details: three to five evidence items for the highlight, under the stage | Pressing Show the details |
| **Beyond** | The article, the résumé, the section | A link in the details |

## 5. Interaction

| Action | Result |
|---|---|
| Next, Previous; a swipe on the stage; Left and Right with the stage focused | The next or previous highlight; at the end, Next goes back to the first (the rail shows where you are) |

## 7. Visual direction

- **The picture is the hero:** about 60% of the stage, edge to edge in its frame, with the site's image shadow; light and dark versions.
- **A quiet stage:** the panel is paper with ink type; the capability tag is the only colour (the indigo spot), and it matches the tag in Jump to and on the rail, so the three read as one system.
- **The rail is text,** not thumbnails, so it doesn't become a second carousel to look at; the current item is filled with the spot.
- **One rhythm:** each highlight has the same anatomy, so the eye learns where to look after the first.

## 8. Motion

| Motion | When | Duration | Reduced motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| The picture and panel slide and fade to the next | Next, Previous, a swipe, a rail or Jump to press | About 300 ms | Instant swap |
| The current rail item's fill moves along | With the change | About 200 ms | Instant |
| The details open | Show the details | About 250 ms | Instant |

Nothing advances on its own, so Spotlight needs no exception to [visual language §6](../site-ui/visual-language.md).

## 9. Accessibility

- The stage follows the WAI-ARIA carousel pattern: a region with `aria-roledescription="carousel"` named "Highlights"; each highlight a group with `aria-roledescription="slide"` named "2 of 8: Defining Microsoft Family".
- Because nothing rotates, there's no rotation control; the slide's container is a polite live region, so a change made with Next is announced.
- Previous and Next are buttons with their names; the rail and Jump to are lists of buttons, with `aria-current="true"` on the current one.
- Swipes always have their buttons; Left and Right only while the stage has focus, never taken from the page.
- Highlights not on stage are in the page's HTML, and reachable without a script: the rail's items are links to `#h-<id>`.

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Only one highlight is at L2 at a time, so a visitor may stop at the first | The rail and Jump to name everything; Next is large; this is why Spotlight isn't the default |
| A capability with one highlight seems smaller than one with two | Jump to gives every capability the same weight; the owner's order mixes them |
| Résumé-only highlights look thin on a big stage | The stage carries a voice and Ask me about it; proof pages replace them as they're written |
| Visitors expect it to move | A visible "2 of 8" and large Next say what to do |
