# More ideas

Seven views are designed in full. These are the other ideas, each tested against the same failure case: does a hiring manager see all six capabilities without doing anything? Three are worth building later, in some form: **Career river**, **Two sides** and **The short version**. Two are about views that already exist or are planned: **Little planet** and **Constellation**. A few more were considered and set aside. They are listed at the end with the reasons. This page is part of [Home page views](README.md) and reads the [capability spine](foundation.md).

> **TL;DR.**
> - **The short version is the best of these:** a one-page brief, led by capabilities, to print or forward. It's what a recruiter sends a hiring manager, and the same page is the site's link preview. Build it in phase 2.
> - **Career river** tells the career as one line, with the six capabilities as strands that join it where the résumé first shows them. Better as the top of the résumé than as a home view.
> - **Two sides** splits the home page into the leader and the maker, after Diego Baca's "rare combination". It's strong at L1 and cheap, so test it against Bento in the preference test.
> - **Little planet** needs one change: a line in Prabin's welcome, and an item in its menu, for a hiring visitor who arrives there by mistake.
> - **Constellation** stays a way to explore. Making it a home view would bend the modes contract, and [Keywords](keywords.md) already covers a field of things to pick.

## 1. How these were judged

Every idea answers the same four questions as the seven views:

1. **L1:** Are all six capabilities on its first screen, at 1280 px and at 360 px, with no action?
2. **Signal:** Can a hiring manager tell, in a few seconds, which capability to dig into?
3. **Impact:** Does it make a first impression the other views don't?
4. **Cost:** What does it need beyond the spine?

## 2. Career river

### 2.1 The idea

Hiring managers read careers as stories: where someone started, what changed, where they are now. The river draws the career as one line, from 2008 to today. Six strands, one per capability, join the line where the résumé first shows them. So the visitor sees both the path and the range at once. The strands also answer a question the other views can't: *where did this come from?* The craft started in motorcycle surfacing, and the systems started in email guidelines.

### 2.2 The three levels

| Level | In Career river |
|---|---|
| **L1** | The four employers and their years on the line; the six strands, each labelled, joining where they begin |
| **L2** | A stop pressed: the role, its years, and one proof line per strand that runs through it |
| **L3** | The stop's résumé lines and its pages (Bento's drawer content) |

**The stops and strands.** Every stop and every strand comes from the résumé.

| Stop | Role and years | Strands that start here |
|---|---|---|
| TVS Motor | Designer, August 2008 to March 2013 | **Craft** (Class-A surfacing, vehicle graphics); **Making** (a presentation-management system he built himself for motorcycle design reviews) |
| Projectplace | UX Designer, March 2013 to June 2015 | **Systems** (product-wide email guidelines and templates) |
| Belong | Head of Design, July 2015 to 2019 | **Leadership** (the design org, built from scratch); **Strategy** (the Core Leadership Team, reporting to the CEO) |
| DesignUp (alongside) | Core founding team, 2016 to now | A side stream joining at 2016: the community, and the events' creative and technical production |
| Microsoft | Principal Design Manager, July 2019 to now | **AI** (the AI transformation program for Design) |

### 2.3 Layout

- **1280 px:** the line runs left to right across the screen. The years are marked under it. The six strands come in from below and join where they start, each in its own lane and labelled with its capability. At the right, "Now" holds the positioning line, Read résumé and Contact me. A pressed stop opens its L2 card above the line.
- **360 px:** the line runs down the page. The years are on the left. The strands are labelled chips that join at their stops. The newest stop comes first, so Microsoft and AI lead. The six strand labels also sit as a row under the name, so L1 holds on the first screen.

### 2.4 Verdict

The river is honest and memorable, and it's the only idea that shows *depth over time*. But it's ordered by time, not by what the hiring manager needs. The most relevant work (AI, Windows) sits at the end of the line on a wide screen. As the home page, it's weaker than Bento at L2. **Recommendation:** make it the top of the résumé page, where a chronology is expected. Offer it as a home view only if the preference test asks for it.

## 3. Two sides

### 3.1 The idea

Diego Baca wrote of "a rare combination of strong leadership, design craft, and hands-on technical ability". Two sides makes that combination the layout. The home page is split down the middle. **Leader** is on the left: Leadership, Strategy, Systems. **Maker** is on the right: Making, Craft. **AI** sits on the seam, because it's both a program he leads and tools he builds. A visitor drags the divider, or presses Leader or Maker, to give one side most of the screen.

### 3.2 The three levels

| Level | In Two sides |
|---|---|
| **L1** | At rest, the divider is centred: three capabilities on each side, AI on the seam, all six labelled |
| **L2** | One side widened: its capabilities' proof lines and pictures (Leader: the Belong org, Windows, Microsoft Family; Maker: Cloner Pro, Atiya, Slate) |
| **L3** | A capability pressed: its evidence (Bento's drawer content) |

### 3.3 Layout

- **1280 px:** two full-height halves under the identity strip. The leader side is on paper, set in Fraunces. The maker side is on a darker scheme, with the work's own pictures (a subtree switched with `color-scheme`, never a branch on the theme). The quote runs across the seam at the top.
- **360 px:** the halves stack: Leader, then the seam with AI and the quote, then Maker. Leader and Maker become two buttons at the top that scroll to their half. All six labels are on the first screen as two rows of three, with AI between them.
- **The divider** is a slider (`role="slider"`, arrow keys move it in steps, Home and End go to either side). The two buttons do the same in one press, so dragging is never required (WCAG 2.5.7).

### 3.4 Verdict

Two sides is the clearest statement of what's *unusual* about the profile. It's strong at L1 and cheap: one layout over the spine. Its risk is the split itself. A hiring manager for a pure leadership role may read "maker" as "not senior", and the reverse. **Recommendation:** a candidate view after phase 2. Put it in the preference test against Bento, with the leadership lens (`?for=leader`) opening it widened to Leader.

## 4. The short version

### 4.1 The idea

A recruiter's next step is to forward the profile. Today they forward a link to a whole site, or a résumé ordered by time. The short version is a one-page brief led by capabilities, made to be printed, saved as PDF, or pasted into an email. It's the same spine at L1 and L2, on paper.

### 4.2 What's on it

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Head | The name, the title, the positioning line, the years of experience |
| Six capabilities | Each one line: its name, its proof line, and one fact with its source |
| Three voices | One short quote each from Diego Baca, March Roger and Prabu Ambravaneswaran |
| Foot | Read résumé, Contact me and the site's address, as links and as printed text |

It fits one A4 or Letter page, in print and on screen, at about 250 words.

### 4.3 Where it lives

- **Its own page,** `/brief/`, with Print and Copy link. It's linked from the identity strip's menu in every view, and from Change view as "The short version".
- **The link preview:** the same content, drawn at build time as the site's preview image, so a link pasted into a chat or an email already shows the six capabilities.
- **Under a lens,** it keeps the lens's order (`/brief/?for=ai`), so a recruiter forwards the version for the role.

### 4.4 Verdict

The short version is cheap. It has no interaction, and its print stylesheet is shared with [Proof grid](proof-grid.md). It also works *after* the visit, which no view does. **Recommendation:** build it in phase 2 with Cover and Proof grid. Telemetry: `brief_open`, `brief_print` and `brief_copy`, each with the lens.

## 5. Little planet's part

[Little planet](../desktop/modes.md) is for the general audience and a memorable second visit. It isn't a home view, and its weak L1 is expected ([README §6](README.md)). The risk is a hiring manager who reaches it first, from a shared link or out of curiosity, and leaves without the map. Two small changes cover that. Both are owner's copy, since the welcome is Prabin's own:

- **The welcome** (it's on the page from its first paint, before any game code loads) gains one line for hiring visitors, with a button, Read short version. A slow phone shows it too.
- **The menu** gains Read résumé, next to the classic site.

The planet's buildings stay the site's sections. Nothing about capabilities is added to the world.

## 6. Constellation

[Constellation](../desktop/modes.md) is the engine's planned reference mode: the sections as stars and their pages as satellites, in 2D, with no WebGL. It's tempting to make it a capability constellation for the home page, with the six capabilities as stars and their evidence as satellites. But a mode only receives the site's sections and pages (`ModeData`), not the spine. Bending it would make the contract planet-shaped in a different way. [Keywords](keywords.md) already designs the field of things to pick, with a proper L1. **Recommendation:** Constellation stays a way to explore, offered beside Little planet.

## 7. Considered and set aside

| Idea | Why not |
|---|---|
| **A chat-first home**, an assistant that answers about him | No server to ground it, and an answer the owner hasn't signed off could invent a claim. The honest version is Questions' Ask something else, with a grounded answer later ([Questions](questions.md), A2) |
| **A scroll story**, one long page with pinned scenes | L1 arrives only at the end, and hijacked scrolling fights the visitor. The Showreel tells the story with controls |
| **A desktop metaphor**, windows and icons | A novelty that hides each capability behind an icon: low L1 |
| **A command line** | Clever for engineers, wrong for recruiters and most hiring managers |
| **A wall of work thumbnails** | Shows the making and hides the leadership and strategy: the failure case |
| **A range map**, a 2D plot from making to leading, detail to organisation | The axes need explaining before the plot reads. Two sides makes the same point with one axis |
| **A page per job description**, generated | Duplicates content, and drifts. The role lens reorders the one spine instead |
