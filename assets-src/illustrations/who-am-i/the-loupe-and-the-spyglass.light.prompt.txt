Create one original editorial illustration. Black ink on clean white paper, light mode.

REFERENCE ROLES: The FIRST reference is the house style anchor. Match its pen work, hatching density, creature design language, level of detail and humour exactly, but NOT its characters or its scene: never draw its explorer, its stage or its books unless the cast below asks for them. The remaining references guide the drawing language only: the sheet of creatures shows the creature grammar (huge, slightly mismatched round eyes with dot pupils, very simple asymmetrical bodies, spindly limbs, expressive mouths, comically surprised or deadpan faces); the bird and cliff drawings guide energetic, imperfect, fast pen strokes with varied pressure, loose contours and expressive hatching. Never draw their bird, tree, cliff or exact animals.

CAST (keep these characters recognisable): THE MAKER, the recurring hero: a small, round, fuzzy creature with huge, slightly mismatched round eyes, a jeweller's loupe screwed into one eye, a battered brass spyglass tucked under one arm, a single curly antenna-like tuft of hair on top of his head, patched dungarees and a tool belt stuffed with pencils, screwdrivers and a tiny hammer; endlessly curious, delighted and a little obsessive. THE SIDEKICK (only where the subject asks for him): a scrappy, hand-built little robot made of tin cans and a dented kettle, with a bendy desk lamp for a head that glows, riveted patches and mismatched bolt eyes; eager, helpful and a little clumsy. THE TOWNSFOLK: tall, solemn, slightly droopy creatures in bowler hats and long buttoned coats, mostly bemused or astonished by the maker.

SUBJECT: A huge, wonderful, ramshackle machine stands in a workshop yard: towers of gears, pipes, chimneys, a big flywheel. High up on its scaffold, the maker hangs upside down by his knees from a scaffold bar. His head and arms are deep inside an open hatch, and through a cutaway we see him peering through his loupe at one tiny cog among hundreds, a tweezer in his hand. At the same time one of his feet sticks up and out of the top of the machine, holding the brass spyglass, pointed at a far horizon of rolling hills, odd towers and a rising sun. A couple of townsfolk on the ground stare up at him, hats tipping back.

VISUAL STORYTELLING: one clear, funny visual metaphor, legible at article width on a phone. Stage it through body language, expressions and props, never through labels. Everyone is an original quirky cartoon creature, never a realistic human, never stock-vector people, never smooth digital outlines or 3D rendering. No readable words, numbers, speech balloons, captions, logos, title, signature or watermark, unless the subject asks for a specific mark.

COMPOSITION: a wide 21:9 landscape lead picture, one coherent scene, no panels. Keep every subject whole and uncropped, use roughly the middle 85 percent of the canvas, with generous untouched white paper around the drawing and only light grounding hatch marks below. No border, no frame, no dense backdrop filling the page.

INK: black ink only. Loose spontaneous hand-drawn pen lines, expressive hatching, irregular broken contours, comic editorial sketch. No colour, no grey wash, no gradients, no tone fills.
