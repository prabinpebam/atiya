Create one original comic strip: exactly THREE equal panels in ONE horizontal row, separated by loose hand-drawn ink panel borders with narrow white gutters. No words, no lettering, no speech balloons, no captions anywhere.

PANEL 1 (left), the convincing conclusion: a muddy forest path with a line of HUGE, deep, three-toed footprints with sharp claw drag marks, sunk deep into the mud. Office stakeholder creatures in small ties and lanyards crouch over the tracks with magnifying glasses; one stretches a tape measure across a footprint wider than he is. On a flip chart beside them, one stakeholder has drawn a towering monster with enormous clawed feet, fangs and spines; the others nod, convinced. At the panel's edge the explorer sits calmly on a stump with his journal open and a small knowing smile. He is not surprised and not confused.

PANEL 2 (middle), the new evidence: further along the same path. The explorer has stood up and gently holds back a large fern with one hand, showing the group what lies behind it, with a calm "have a look" expression. The giant footprints stop dead at a pair of enormous hand-carved wooden monster-foot clogs with straps and carved claws, their soles carved in the same three-toed shape, lying abandoned. Beyond the clogs, a line of TINY ordinary little footprints continues down the path. The stakeholders freeze in a row, wide eyes moving from the clogs to the tiny prints; the tape measure droops.

PANEL 3 (right), the reasoning rebuilt: the stakeholders eagerly redraw the flip chart: the big monster is scribbled out and replaced by a small round creature wearing the oversized clogs, with an arrow from the clogs to the deep prints. One stakeholder kneels to carefully measure a tiny footprint. In the corner, the real culprit peeks shyly from behind a tree: a tiny, bashful, barefoot round creature holding a half-carved third clog and a little chisel. The explorer leans against a tree, pleased, ticking a box in his journal.
